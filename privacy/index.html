Nomira

Privacy Policy

Effective November 16, 2026

Nomira is an iPhone app that helps you cut back on screen time. This policy explains what we collect, why, who else handles it, how long we keep it, and how to delete it. "We" means the independent developer who makes Nomira, named as the seller on Nomira's App Store page.

The short version. We collect only what the app needs to work. We never sell your data, never show ads, and never use what you type to train AI models. Nomira is for ages 13 and up. Nomira's AI is for adults only, and it only reads what you type if you say yes. Apple's Screen Time keeps your exact app choices on your iPhone. You can erase what you typed, or delete your whole account, inside the app.

What we collect

What Why
An account ID. A random ID created when you first open the app. If you sign in with Apple, Apple confirms who you are; we don't ask Apple for your name or email. If we offer email sign-in later and you use it, we keep your email address. To keep your plans, progress and subscription together, including on a new phone.
Your age group. You pick an age range when you start. We only keep whether you are a teen (13 to 17) or an adult (18 or older). It is set once and can't be changed in the app. To keep the AI off for teens, and to turn away anyone under 13.
Your plans. Your block schedules (their names, days and times), your daily limit, and a short code for each app, category or website you block. The code can't be turned back into the app's name; the names stay on your iPhone. So your rules hold: making a rule stricter works right away, and loosening it waits until tomorrow. Our server keeps track of that, not your phone.
Your settings. Time zone, the AI's voice, your wait time, up to three goals, an optional short note about yourself, how long to keep what you type (30 or 90 days), and switches such as Commit mode, Strict mode and the slip warning. To run your plans at the right local times and to set up the app the way you chose.
Your AI choice. Whether you let Nomira's AI read what you type at the Door, which AI company you agreed to, and when. So nothing you type goes to the AI unless you agreed.
Door activity. When you tried to open a blocked app and which one (from Nomira's list), the pause you were shown and how it went, any reason or excuse you typed (up to 400 characters), the AI's reply and decision, times you closed the app, waited or used an emergency pass, how long you were let in, and whether you said an unlock was worth it. To make decisions at the Door, enforce your daily limits, learn which pauses work for you, spot the hours you're most likely to slip, and show your progress and streak.
Step counts during a walk. Only while a walk is running, read from your iPhone's motion sensor with your permission. We receive the number of steps, nothing else. To check you walked before giving you time. We don't track your location.
Block health. Whether Screen Time access is still on while a plan is active, and which on-phone checks failed. To tell you when your block may have slipped, and to notice when it's switched off mid-plan.
Subscription status. Whether you have Nomira Pro, whether it is active, and when it ends or renews. Apple handles payment; we never see your card. To unlock the paid features and keep them working on all your devices.
Crash reports and usage events. Device model, iOS version, app version, crash details, and events like "paywall viewed" or "walk finished", linked to your account ID. Events only contain numbers, yes or no answers and fixed labels. To fix crashes and understand which features help people. These never include what you type or the names of apps you block.

This comes from you, from your iPhone (step counts and Screen Time status, with your permission), and from Apple (sign-in and subscription status). We don't collect your contacts, photos, location, browsing history, advertising ID, or the content of other apps.

Screen Time and your app choices

Blocking runs on Apple's Screen Time. When you choose an app in Apple's picker, Apple gives Nomira a sealed token that stays on your iPhone. We can't read it, and it never reaches our server; only a short code made from it does, so your rules know which item is which. Apple's Screen Time decides which apps are blocked on your phone. We don't see how long you spend in other apps or what you do in them.

Face ID and Touch ID

When you delete your account, Nomira asks your iPhone to confirm it's you with Face ID, Touch ID or your passcode. Apple handles this on your phone. Nomira only learns yes or no. We never see or store your face, fingerprint or passcode.

Steps

Step counts are health-related, so we treat them with extra care. We read them only during a walk you start, use them only to check that walk, never use them for ads, and never sell or share them beyond the providers below. You can turn off Motion & Fitness access for Nomira in your iPhone settings at any time; walks then stop working, and everything else keeps working.

How we use it

  • To run the app: your plans, the Door, your limits, walks, progress and subscription.
  • To keep it safe and working: preventing abuse and cheating, fixing crashes, and securing accounts.
  • To improve it: understanding which features help, mostly from counts rather than individual use.
  • To follow the law and respond to lawful requests.

Nomira learns from your own Door history to pick the pauses that work for you and to warn you before the hours you usually slip. This only changes how Nomira works for you. We don't use your data for advertising, and we don't build profiles about you for anyone else.

Nomira's AI (adults only)

Nomira's AI is a computer program, not a person. It is only for adults aged 18 and older. If you are 13 to 17, the AI stays off: our server refuses to send anything a teen types to the AI, whatever is chosen in the app. Everything else in Nomira works the same for teens.

For adults, the AI only reads what you type if you tap Allow on the screen that asks first. You can change this any time in Settings → Privacy. With it off, nothing you type goes to the AI; blocking still works, and you can still walk for time or use your emergency pass.

With it on, when you make your case at the Door (or try it during setup), we send what you typed, the app you're opening, how many tries and unlocks you have left today, and your last few excuses and their outcomes to Anthropic (the company that makes the Claude AI) to get a recommendation. We don't send your name, email, account ID, goals or note about yourself. Nomira's own server then makes the final decision using fixed limits the AI can't change.

Anthropic processes this for us under its commercial terms. It doesn't use it to train its models, and it deletes it within 30 days, except that it may keep messages its safety systems flag (for up to 2 years) or where the law requires. See How the bouncer works and safety.

Analytics, cookies and tracking

The app doesn't use cookies or advertising IDs, and doesn't track you across other companies' apps or websites. Crash reports (Sentry) and usage events (PostHog) are linked only to your random account ID and never include what you type.

Who else handles your data

We use these service providers. Each one only gets what it needs to provide its service to us:

  • Supabase stores the account and app data above (our database and server).
  • Anthropic receives what you type at the Door to suggest the AI's reply, only if you are an adult and turned the AI on.
  • RevenueCat checks your App Store subscription and tells our server whether it is active and when it ends.
  • Apple handles sign-in, payments, Face ID and Touch ID, and Screen Time.
  • Sentry receives crash reports.
  • PostHog receives usage events.

We don't sell or rent your personal information, and we don't share it for targeted advertising. We disclose data only if the law requires it, to protect people's safety, to professional advisers (such as lawyers or accountants) who must keep it confidential, or as part of a sale or transfer of the app, in which case this policy still applies to it.

How long we keep it

  • What you type at the Door (excuses, reasons and the AI's replies) is erased after 90 days, or after 30 days if you choose that in Settings → Privacy. Only counts stay, so your stats work.
  • Erase my Door history in Settings → Privacy erases all of it right away.
  • Unused guest accounts (never signed in, unused for 30 days) are deleted automatically.
  • Everything else is kept until you delete your account. Deleting it (Settings → Delete account) removes your data from our database right away, including your plans and subscription status. Copies in our providers' backups and logs are removed as those expire.
  • Purchase records are also kept by Apple and RevenueCat under their own policies, because they handle the payment. Deleting your account doesn't cancel your subscription; cancel it in your Apple ID settings.
  • At Anthropic, as described in "Nomira's AI" above.

Age and teens

Nomira is for ages 13 and up. The app asks your age range at the start and stops anyone under 13 before any answers are saved. We don't knowingly collect personal information from children under 13. If you believe a child under 13 is using Nomira, contact us and we'll delete the account.

For teens aged 13 to 17:

  • Nomira's AI is always off, and our server enforces it.
  • There are no ads, and we never use a teen's data for ads, sell it, or share it for ads.
  • We use a teen's data only to run the app, keep it safe and fix it, as described above.
  • A parent or guardian can email us to see or delete their teen's data. Apple's own Screen Time and Ask to Buy settings also apply.

Your choices and rights

In the app you can see your history, erase what you typed, turn the AI on or off (adults), and delete your account at any time.

Everyone can also email us to get a copy of their data, correct it, or delete it. We'll confirm the request comes from the account holder, and we respond within 30 days (or sooner where the law requires). We won't treat you differently for using these rights.

United States. Depending on your state (for example California, Colorado, Connecticut, Virginia, Texas or Oregon), you may have the right to know what we collect, to access, correct, delete or get a portable copy of it, and to opt out of its sale, its use for targeted advertising, or profiling. We don't sell your data, share it for targeted advertising, or use it for profiling with legal or similarly significant effects. You can use an authorised agent, and if we turn down a request you can appeal by replying to our answer.

EU, UK and Switzerland. We use your data to provide the app you asked for (contract), to keep it secure and improve it (our legitimate interests), with your consent for the AI and for step counts (which you can withdraw at any time), and to meet legal obligations. You have the rights to access, correct, delete, restrict, object to, and port your data, and to complain to your local data protection authority.

Where your data is processed

We and our providers are based in, or process data in, the United States and other countries whose privacy laws may differ from yours. Where the law requires it, transfers are protected by safeguards such as standard contractual clauses.

Security

Data travels over encrypted connections. Your sign-in is stored encrypted on your phone, with its key in the iPhone Keychain. Our database only lets your account read its own rows, and decisions like unlocking time are made on our server, not on the phone. No system is perfectly secure, but we work to protect your data and will tell you if a breach affects it, as the law requires.
